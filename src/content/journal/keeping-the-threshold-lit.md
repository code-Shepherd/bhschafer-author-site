Speculative fiction works best when it remembers two truths at once: the world is stranger than we can map, and people are still people inside that strangeness.

I keep returning to threshold moments in stories: doors, rituals, borders, first glimpses. Not because they are exotic, but because they force characters to reveal what they fear losing.

When I draft, I ask a simple question: _what must this person protect to stay human?_ If I can answer that, the magic systems, monsters, and symbols begin to arrange themselves naturally around emotional stakes.
